# Handoff candidates

What statcompute has that pprof_py or pprof_spark could take, as of 2026-11-22. The list was drawn
up by the maintainer and the assistant in round 22 and is recorded here in round 23. It is a list
of candidates, not of things handed over: decision D-17 says an algorithm is handed over when a
written specification, a reference kernel that needs no Spark and fixtures with expected values
exist and have been offered, and none has reached that. When one does, it gets a folder here.

Each candidate is stated with the evidence the repository holds and with what is missing. A change
to pprof_py's methodology is reviewed explicitly by its owners (D-12); statcompute proposes and
does not modify either package.

## For pprof_py

| Candidate | Evidence | State | Missing before it is offered |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The stopping-rule fix (RI-1).** `newton_raphson` halves a step while the log-likelihood falls and tests convergence afterwards; at the last iteration the sign of that fall is rounding, so a fit can report convergence up to a whole Newton step short of the root | `docs/reference-issues.md`, RI-1; `docs/research/reference-stopping-rule.md`; counted on the cluster: at 5 million rows by 100 features the third step was halved six times, 4.34e-5 standard errors from the root with `converged` true (`docs/reports/2026-11-21-databricks-warm-runs.md`, section 1) | **Ready to send.** The text is `docs/reference-reports/RI-1-pprof_py-halved-last-step.md`. Sending it is the maintainer's to do | Nothing |
| **The NumPy Cox kernel in weighted-Gram form.** The information as one weighted product of the feature matrix less rank-one terms, instead of second moments carried along the event times | On one machine at 5 million rows the prototype `lab_numpy` fitted in 55.1 and 60.3 seconds at 50 features and 95.2 and 85.4 at 100, where pprof_py took 87.6 and 82.9, and 202.3 and 200.7: 1.4 to 1.6 times faster at 50 features and 2.1 to 2.4 at 100 (`docs/reports/2026-11-16-databricks-compare-5m.md`, section 3). Its estimates were within 1.9e-10 standard errors of pprof_py's | Measured once, **not on equal cores**: `lab_numpy` could use all 8 cores of the driver through BLAS, pprof_py uses one thread (the same report, section 5). The prototype covers Breslow ties and right censoring only; the engine's kernel (`statcompute/engine/kernels.py`, run on one machine by `statcompute.engine.local`) also covers Efron ties, entry times, weights and offsets, and is 27 to 39 percent slower than the prototype's because it centres the features (probe A-12, `docs/research/pass-cost.md`) | A timing with BLAS held to one thread, and of the engine's kernel rather than the prototype's; 180 features; the specification is `docs/spec/engine.md`, the fixtures are not made |
| **The two-pass algorithm for Cox without strata.** Risk sums on a grid of the distinct times, so that no partition needs the rows in time order: one pass for the tables, one for the products | `docs/spec/engine.md`, section 2.2; `tests/test_engine_time.py`, against pprof_py within the tolerance classes; the prior-art search (`docs/research/prior-art-time-grid.md`: the algebra is coxdev's, the arrangement for distributed data was not found elsewhere). On the cluster the engine's whole call took 17.7 and 18.5 seconds at best at 5 million rows (`docs/reports/2026-11-21-databricks-warm-runs.md`, section 4) | **Not measured on one machine.** `statcompute.engine.local` fits the same model from arrays with the same kernels, and has not been timed against pprof_py. Limits: at most 20,000 distinct times, one stratum (OI-23) | The one-machine timing against pprof_py at 5 million rows; whether pprof_py, which holds the data in memory and sorts once, gains anything from it at all |
| **Stopping on the Newton step in standard errors.** A fit stops when the step it would take next is below a tolerance in units of the standard errors (T_CONV, decision D-05), not on the relative change of the log-likelihood | `statcompute/engine/newton.py`; `docs/spec/engine.md`, section 5; RI-1 shows what the other rule costs: the estimate depends on the path, and a difference between two exact fits can be the rule's and not the arithmetic's (D-09) | Used by the engine and both prototypes in every run. For pprof_py it is a change of methodology, not a repair: its estimates would move by up to its present stopping error | The owners' review. It is independent of RI-1, which repairs the present rule without replacing it |

## For pprof_spark

| Candidate | Evidence | State | Missing before it is offered |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The two-pass algorithm for Cox without strata** | As above. pprof_spark ran such a fit as one task; in plan `unstratified_5m` at 5 million rows its attempts ended in a stage failure after 273 to 916 seconds (RI-8, `docs/reference-issues.md`) | **RI-8 was seen on an earlier build that was not the pinned commit, and has not been retested.** The engine fits the same data in 17.7 to 18.5 seconds at best | A run of `unstratified_5m` with the pinned build `b92919c`; then the specification and fixtures |
| **Fewer, larger tasks.** One task per core instead of many small ones | For the engine's Python tasks: at 5 million rows on 40 cores, 40 tasks per pass made an evaluation 17 to 28 percent shorter than 100, and 20 were no better than 100 (`docs/reports/2026-11-21-databricks-partition-size.md`; decision D-21; again on 2026-11-22). pprof_spark runs 3,800 to 15,600 tasks per fit at this size, in stages of 515 and 992 tasks whose median task takes a tenth of a second | **A hypothesis for JVM tasks.** What a task costs was measured for tasks that start a Python worker and receive their rows through Arrow; a JVM task has neither cost | A measurement in pprof_spark itself. Its code must be read first (OI-37) |
| **The design of the logistic fit.** The provider effects profiled out inside each partition, so that an evaluation is one pass and the Newton iteration is over the coefficients alone | `docs/spec/engine.md`, section 3; the engine's whole call is 2.8 to 4.5 times shorter than pprof_spark's for the logistic model at 5 million rows (`docs/reports/2026-11-22-databricks-default-partitions.md`, section 4) | **Needs the code re-read first.** What `docs/capabilities.md` says of pprof_spark's logistic fit (two passes per step and two more for the variance) was read at commit `e5c9484`; the pinned build is `b92919c` (OI-37). The timings compare whole implementations, not designs | OI-37; then a statement of the difference in the two packages' own terms |
| **The benchmark harness and the warm-up protocol.** One record per fit with the cluster sampled during it; the stored table read and the Python workers started before the clock (D-18, D-20); the executors asked for and counted before every fit; caveats on the runs that are not fair timings | `statcompute/bench/`; `docs/benchmark-protocol.md`; every report under `docs/reports/` | In use since 2026-11-16. It already runs pprof_spark through an adapter. The warm-up covers nothing in the JVM: pprof_spark's slowest task was 3.3 to 4.6 seconds in 8 of its 16 calls of 2026-11-22 | Whether pprof_spark's project wants it: it has a benchmark of its own |
| **The flag-stability check and the tolerance classes.** Two computations of one fit may flag a provider differently only when its statistic is closer to the cut-off than the computations are to each other (D-15); named tolerances for coefficients, variances and log-likelihoods | `statcompute/validation/flags.py`; `statcompute/testkit/tolerances.py`; `docs/spec/reproducibility.md`. On the cluster the engine flagged the same providers in all 12 runs of each logistic dataset, across three versions and layouts of 100, 40 and 24 partitions | In use. T_FN, T_COEF and T_VAR follow pprof_spark's own classes; the flag check is statcompute's. pprof_spark repeats bit for bit by design, so for it the check matters when its results are set beside another implementation's, not between its own runs | The harness compares flags between runs of the engine only (OI-28) |

## What the list is not

- It does not rank the candidates. The maintainer's order of work is in `STATUS.md`.
- It does not say that any of them is better for the package it is offered to. Several rest on
  timings of whole implementations in one or two sessions, and two are marked as not measured
  where they would be applied: the two-pass algorithm on one machine, and fewer tasks in the JVM.
- Nothing has been sent. RI-1 is the first, and it is a report of a defect, not an algorithm.
