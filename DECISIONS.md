# Decisions

Proposed by the assistant; taken by the maintainer. Numbers are never reused.

| ID | Decision | Status |
|---|---|---|
| D-01 | The repository is named `statcompute`. It holds the research workspace, the benchmark of all implementations and the new engine | Approved 2026-11-14; pushed to https://github.com/taoxu2016/statcompute |
| D-02 | Licence: MIT, as the reference packages | Approved 2026-11-14. LICENSE added in round 3; the copyright holder is Tao Xu |
| D-03 | Target runtime: Databricks Runtime 17.3 LTS, which is Apache Spark 4.0.0 with Scala 2.13. pprof_spark targets Spark 4.1.0 and Runtime 18 | Approved 2026-11-14 |
| D-04 | The engine's language and determinism are decided after the baseline, not before. Two candidates are measured side by side in step 0: JVM kernels with bitwise partition invariance (pprof_spark's rule NN-4, the feasibility study's route), and NumPy kernels inside Spark partitions (`lab_spark`), which are simpler and use BLAS but are not bitwise reproducible | Approved 2026-11-14; the choice itself is still open |
| D-05 | Prototypes stop when the next Newton step is below 1e-8 standard errors (1e-10 for "tight"): the tolerance class T_CONV of the research roadmap (F-10). The bound is provisional until calibrated | Approved 2026-11-14 |
| D-06 | Numerical comparisons take pprof_py at its tightest tolerance as the reference | Approved 2026-11-14 |
| D-07 | The repository stores run records and reports of synthetic data only, as JSON and Markdown. Since the repository is public, records are scrubbed when written: the cluster identifier is hashed, and e-mail addresses and user folders are removed from messages and traces | Approved 2026-11-14; the scrubbing was added in round 2 |

| D-08 | The engine is Python on Spark: NumPy kernels inside partitions that hold whole strata or providers (`statcompute.engine`). This settles D-04. Reasons: on the production cluster the prototype of this design gave the same estimates as pprof_py and was faster than pprof_spark for the logistic model; its kernels use memory outside the 2.2 GB JVM heaps that Photon leaves; it needs no JAR build; it can be tested end to end in the assistant's sandbox; and pprof_spark's own project is already pursuing the JVM route. Cost: results are reproducible bit for bit only for a fixed layout and session, and to rounding otherwise | Approved 2026-11-21. Proposed 2026-11-16; the engine has been built on it since its first version |
| D-09 | A Cox estimate agrees with pprof_py's when the two differ by the Newton step that remains at pprof_py's estimate; the harness is to report that step beside the difference. Reason: pprof_py halves its last step whenever rounding makes the log-likelihood come out lower, at any tolerance, so D-06 does not make its estimate the root (`docs/spec/engine.md`, section 5.3; 4.3e-5 standard errors on the cluster's 100-feature data). The tests have compared this way since round 5. Cost: one evaluation at the reference's estimate per comparison; until the harness has it, its agreement table shows such a fit as over T_COEF | Approved 2026-11-21. Proposed 2026-11-19. The harness does not report the remaining step yet (OI-34); from round 17 an agreement row says how many steps the reference halved |
| D-10 | Without strata the working set is laid out like the other two: ceil(rows / `rows_per_partition`) partitions, the rows placed by row identifier and sorted by it. Reasons: the fit no longer inherits the input's partitions (17 and 33 on the cluster, for 40 cores), and with `row_id` it returns the same bits whatever the input's layout. Cost: a shuffle and a sort when the working set is built, which a fit without strata did not have, and one pass-1 table per partition to the driver in every evaluation (OI-26). `partitions="input"` keeps the earlier layout | Approved 2026-11-21, and amended the same day by D-19: the layout stays, on request; the default without strata is the input's partitions again. Proposed 2026-11-19; rounds 12 to 17 had it as the default. The run this entry named as the test of its default went against it (`docs/reports/2026-11-21-databricks-warm-runs.md`, section 4) |
| D-11 | Times the engine reports: "prepare" ends when the working set exists, so for Cox it contains the first evaluation, which is the pass that builds it; "fit" is the evaluations after it; every evaluation's time is recorded. Reason: one job fewer means the build has no job of its own to time. Cost: "fit" in a Cox record from round 12 on is one evaluation shorter than in earlier records, so across versions only whole calls compare | Approved 2026-11-21. Proposed 2026-11-19; round 12 implements it |
| D-12 | pprof_py remains the statistical specification, but it is not unquestionable computational ground truth. statcompute validates results independently and reports potential issues; a change of methodology is reviewed explicitly | The maintainer's, 2026-11-20. Issues found are kept in `docs/reference-issues.md`. D-06 is read with this: pprof_py at its tightest tolerance is what estimates are compared with, not the definition of their last digits |
| D-13 | Algorithms flow from statcompute to both pprof_py and pprof_spark; validation and benchmark evidence flow back. The goal: statcompute is a reusable platform for computational research and development whose validated algorithms benefit both packages | The maintainer's, 2026-11-20, with the division of responsibilities now in `docs/architecture.md` |
| D-14 | Bitwise reproducibility is not an absolute requirement. Agreement within strict numerical tolerances, together with stable provider-level flags and auditable execution configurations, may be sufficient | The maintainer's, 2026-11-20. It removes the main cost recorded against D-08, which stays the maintainer's to take. D-15 and D-16 propose what the second and the third condition mean |
| D-15 | Stable provider-level flags, defined. Two computations of the same fit may flag a provider differently only when its two statistics are within T_FLAG of each other: 1e-7 for a z statistic, 1e-9 for a p-value. Such a provider is borderline and is reported; any other difference in a flag is a numerical disagreement. A run record keeps, per test, the counts, a digest of the flags and the smallest distance of a statistic from its cut-off. Reason: a flag is a cut-off on a continuous statistic, so no tolerance on the statistic can promise equal flags, and among 8,000 providers and many runs one will sit on a cut-off some day. Evidence: probe A-10. Not decided here: whether a production report shows borderline providers, which is methodology | Approved 2026-11-21. Proposed 2026-11-20. `statcompute.validation.flags` computes it, with T_FLAG_Z and T_FLAG_P among the tolerance classes; the engine's logistic records carry the summary. On the cluster on 2026-11-21: the same providers flagged in all four runs of both logistic datasets |
| D-16 | The execution record, defined: what is kept with a fit so that its digits can be audited. The data's identity and row count; the package version; every setting of the estimator; the layout (partitions, rows per partition, how rows are placed, the row identifier, the Arrow batch size); Spark's version and runtime; Python, NumPy and the BLAS library on the driver and on the executors; iterations, evaluations and the last Newton step | Approved 2026-11-21. Proposed 2026-11-20. `docs/spec/reproducibility.md` says what the harness records already and what is missing (OI-29) |
| D-17 | A last stage for an algorithm after "engine": handed over. It leaves statcompute as a written specification, a reference kernel that needs no Spark, and fixtures with expected values, offered to the maintainers of pprof_py and pprof_spark, who apply it. And a register of issues found in the packages this project reads, with the evidence and whether each has been reported | Approved 2026-11-21. Proposed 2026-11-20. The stage is in `docs/architecture.md`; the register is `docs/reference-issues.md`, with eight entries |
| D-18 | Timings that compare implementations or versions are taken with the stored table already read once by the executors (plan option `warm_input`), and the cost of that read is recorded beside them. Reason: on the standard cluster an executor's first read of a stored file stalls one task for 17 to 21 seconds in whichever job comes first, in every implementation; six of the eight calls of round 12 with strata or providers were stalled that way, which hid everything else. Cost: a timing no longer includes a cold read, which a first production run on fresh executors does pay; the report must therefore keep both | Approved 2026-11-21. Proposed 2026-11-20; the option exists since round 15. On the cluster on 2026-11-21 the light job took 0.6 to 1.8 seconds in all 24 Cox calls, and the read before the clock 11 to 37 seconds before 17 of 32 calls. The plans named `..._warm` set it; the earlier results folders hold runs without it |
| D-19 | Without strata the engine leaves the rows in the input's own partitions by default; the layout of round 12 (rows placed by row identifier and sorted, which repeats bit for bit) is given on request, by a number of partitions or by `partitions="rows"`. Reason: the run D-10 named as its test. At a steady ten executors the input's 17 and 33 partitions gave whole calls of 17.7 and 18.5 seconds at best, the fastest fits without strata seen, where 100 partitions by row identifier gave 29 and 37; every later evaluation is slower with them, by 0.6 seconds at 50 features and 2.3 at 100, and the working set costs a shuffle and a sort. By D-14 identical bits are not required. Cost: a fit then has as many tasks per pass as the input has partitions, few for a table stored in few files, and repeats to rounding only | Approved 2026-11-21. Round 18 implements it: `partitions` unset or `"input"` leaves the rows as stored, a number or `"rows"` places them by row identifier. The configuration that is now the default ran on the cluster as `partitions="input"`; version 0.16.0 itself has not run there |
| D-20 | The read before the clock (D-18) also starts the Python workers: one short task on every executor that imports what the kernels import. Reason: with the stored table read first, half of the timed calls on the cluster (16 of 32) still had one task of 6.5 to 10.5 seconds in the pass that builds the working set, 12 of them on an executor attached 44 to 128 seconds before; the first evaluation then takes 12 to 17 seconds instead of 5 to 9. The cluster replaces its executors all the time: their identifiers reached 74 in one session of a cluster of ten. Cost: as D-18, a timing then says less about a first run on fresh executors; for an implementation whose kernels run in the JVM nothing comparable is warmed | Approved 2026-11-21. Round 18 implements it as part of `warm_input`: one short job whose tasks import NumPy, pandas, pyarrow and SciPy on every executor and report their libraries, which is also the executors' part of the execution record (D-16). Untried on a cluster |
| D-21 | With strata or providers the working set has, by default, one partition for every core the cluster has when the fit starts, and more only when a partition would then hold more than 200 MB of features (rows times features times 8 bytes). `rows_per_partition` or `partitions`, when given, fix the layout as before, independently of the cluster. Reason: on the cluster a pass costs for its tasks as well as for its rows. At 5 million rows on 40 cores, 40 tasks per pass made later evaluations 17 to 28 percent shorter than the 100 of the present default, and 20 tasks were no better than 100, half the cores being idle (`docs/reports/2026-11-21-databricks-partition-size.md`). 200 MB is the largest partition that has run: 250,000 rows by 100 features. Cost: the default layout then depends on the size of the cluster at the moment of the fit, so two fits of the same data on clusters of different size, or on an autoscaling one at different moments, add in different orders and agree to rounding, not bit for bit; D-14 allows it, and the record of a fit names its partitions (D-16). The evidence is one size of data on one cluster. Alternative: keep the layout independent of the cluster and raise the default to 125,000 rows per partition, which is the same at 5 million rows on 40 cores and nowhere else | Approved 2026-11-21. Round 22 implements it (`working_partitions` in `statcompute/engine/spark.py`): the model's settings and its `execution` say which rule gave the number, and the cores. The layout it gives at 5 million rows on 40 cores ran on the cluster as `engine_125k` on 2026-11-21. The default itself ran there on 2026-11-22: one partition per core at the start in all 16 fits, 40 on ten executors and 24 on six, and evaluations as long as with 40 tasks before (`docs/reports/2026-11-22-databricks-default-partitions.md`). Partitions of 200 MB on every core of an executor at once have not run either (OI-41) |
| D-22 | Every plan that holds the cluster at a size asks again when the executors are short just before a timed fit: the option `hold_again`: 1 in `compare_5m`, `compare_5m_warm`, `engine_partitions_5m_warm`, `unstratified_5m`, `unstratified_5m_warm` and `unstratified_5m_warm_input`. Reason: the harness asks for ten executors, counts ten, reads the table and starts Python, and the fit then runs on six: 7 of the 64 fits of 2026-11-22, and 11 in what survives of the earlier sessions, none flagged (`docs/reports/2026-11-22-databricks-default-partitions.md`, section 5). Each loss costs two fits, the one on six executors and the next, which starts on four new ones and takes roughly 4 to 7 seconds longer. Asking again before the fit keeps the first of the two; the new executors are then read in and have Python started before the clock, as D-18 and D-20 have it. Cost: the time the cluster takes to give four executors back, paid before that fit instead of before the next (how long it took is in the records, not in the reports), and up to the 15 minutes of the warm-up's limit when the cluster does not give them; as with D-18 and D-20, a timing then says less about what a single call meets on an autoscaling cluster. It does nothing for a loss during a fit. Alternative: leave the plans as they are and rely on the caveat, which from version 0.21.0 marks these fits and takes them out of the medians without caveat, at the price of the fits lost | Proposed 2026-11-22. Round 23 implements the option and gives it to the new plan `unstratified_cores_5m_warm` only. The other plans are unchanged until the maintainer rules. Counting the executors just before the fit, recording the count and flagging a short one are in every plan from version 0.21.0: they change what is recorded, not what is timed |
